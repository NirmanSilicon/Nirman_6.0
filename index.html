        About

        The tools exist. The conversation is still broken.

        Captions, OCR, scene description and phrase boards all work well on their own. The person still has to move between them, and the other side of the conversation has no shared way to answer.

        Sangam does not replace those tools. It connects a chosen input to a chosen output, for both people at once, with a correction step in the middle.

        How it works

        Four steps, every message

        1. 1

          Input

          Mic, keyboard, phrase board or camera.

        2. 2

          Process

          Speech to text, typed text or text read from a photo.

        3. 3

          Adapt

          Match what the other person chose to receive.

        4. 4

          Output

          Captions, a spoken reply, or text read aloud.

        If recognition is shaky, Sangam marks the text as uncertain and the sender fixes it before it goes out. Nothing is sent behind the user's back.

        Sound alerts

        SANGAM listens through your microphone for alarms, sirens, doorbells and vehicle horns, and tells you on screen, by flashing, by vibration or out loud, in the ways you choose.

        Read this first

        • This is not a safety device. It can miss sounds and it can raise false alarms. Do not use it in place of certified smoke or fire alarms, flashing or vibrating alarm kits, or a person who can alert you.
        • It only works while this page is open and the microphone is on. Phones and laptops may pause the microphone when the screen is off or the tab is in the background.
        • Private by default. Sound is analysed on this device and thrown away straight away. It is never recorded, stored or uploaded. The microphone indicator below stays on while SANGAM listens, and Stop turns it off at once.
        • How it decides. A Google sound-recognition model (YAMNet) scores each second of audio against 521 everyday sound types. Its scores are not probabilities, so "confidence" is the model's raw score for the sound it thinks it heard.
        Microphone is off.

        Your browser will ask for the microphone when you press Start. The sound model (about 26 MB) loads first, so you need the SANGAM server reachable the first time.

        Sounds to watch for

        Sensitivity

        How to alert me

        One alert is shown when a sound starts. A sound that keeps going does not alert again; the alert stays on screen and says "Still sounding". A new alert for the same sound type can only appear after 4 seconds, and only once it has been quiet for 2 seconds.

        Loud sound, not identified (off by default)

        This is not sound classification: it only measures how loud the microphone is, with no idea what caused it. It only ever fires when none of the sound types above has already recognised the noise, and every alert it shows says plainly that it is unidentified. Turn it on if you also want to know about a loud sound that is not an alarm, siren, doorbell, horn, knock, cry or breaking glass — an earlier teammate's loudness-only prototype worked this way for every sound; here it is a fallback for what classification misses, not a replacement for it.

        Check that it works

        Preview buttons show how an alert looks and feels. They are simulated and are labelled that way. They do not test the microphone or the model.

        To test real detection, press Start listening, then play a 4 second smoke-alarm style beep through your speaker. Your microphone has to hear it, so turn the volume up a little.

        Sign Language Camera

        Sign in front of your camera. SANGAM matches what it sees against a vocabulary and shows the words for you to check before anything is spoken or sent.

        Read this first

        • This is not a full sign-language translator. It recognises single signs from a vocabulary. It does not understand sentences, grammar, facial expression or body posture.
        • Indian and American Sign Language use real pretrained models (AI4Bharat OpenHands, trained on the INCLUDE and WLASL datasets). They each recognise one isolated sign at a time from their own fixed vocabulary. SANGAM has not independently tested their accuracy with real signers — see docs/SIGN_LANGUAGE_MODEL_EVALUATION.md before relying on them.
        • What else works: real hand tracking in your browser, plus a recogniser you teach with your own signs. It recognises your performances of the signs you record.
        • Private by default. Camera pictures are processed on this device and are never uploaded or recorded. Only the text you confirm and send leaves your device.

          Sign Language Camera

          Sign in front of your camera. Check the words before you speak or send them.

          Before the camera opens

          • Your camera will be used to follow your hands and match your signs. Nothing is recorded or uploaded; pictures are processed in this browser and discarded.
          • Only the text you confirm and send goes to the other person.
          • Recognition can be wrong. Always check the words before speaking or sending them.
          • You can choose a pretrained Indian or American Sign Language word model, spell words letter by letter with the American fingerspelling alphabet, or teach SANGAM your own signs. None of these has been tested by SANGAM with real signers.
          • The microphone is not used.
          Camera on

          Keep both hands and your upper body in view. Good light on your hands helps.

          Teach a sign

          Give a sign a name (the word it should produce), press Record, then perform the sign once. Record at least 2 examples of each sign, ideally 3 to 5, at different speeds. Examples are saved on this device only.

            Teach a held pose (one hand, hold still)

            For a hand shape you can hold still, such as a gesture you and the people you talk to already use. Name it, press Capture, then hold the shape still in front of the camera until the capture finishes. Teach at least two different poses, capturing each three or more times (move your hand a little closer, further or tilted between captures). Use the same hand every time. These are your own gestures: they are not Indian Sign Language, American Sign Language or any other language. Only numbers (hand positions) are saved on this device, never pictures. Choose “My held poses” in the language list to use them.
